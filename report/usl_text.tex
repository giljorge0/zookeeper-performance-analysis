We fitted the USL to 39 measurements of a single configuration (3 servers, write ratio 0.3): 13 client counts from 1 to 160, at least 3 replicates each, run in random order. The fit is weighted by relative error and the intervals are 95\% bootstrap intervals over the replicates.

The ideal single-client throughput is $\gamma=2082$ ops/s (CI 1996--2169) and the contention coefficient is $\alpha=0.038$ (CI 0.035--0.041), so the contention term alone limits the speedup over one client to $1/\alpha\approx26.0$ and caps throughput at about $\gamma/\alpha\approx54154$ ops/s.

A coherency term is \emph{not} supported: the contention-only (Amdahl) model fits as well (AIC -241.4 against -239.5 for the full USL; F-test $p=0.72$), so we do not claim retrograde scaling and report $\alpha$ only. The best measured throughput is 46436 ops/s at 160 clients, and the curve still rises at the largest load.

As a validation, a fit using only $N\le64$ predicts the held-out higher loads with a mean absolute error of 4.0\%.
